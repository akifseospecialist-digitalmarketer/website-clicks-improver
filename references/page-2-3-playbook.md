# Page 2-3 Playbook - pushing pages from positions 11-30 onto page 1

Read this before writing the **push-to-page-1 plan** for the Page 2-3 Pages tab.

Pages at 11-30 already have relevance. Google associates them with the query; they just aren't the best answer
yet. The plan should be **3-6 specific, ordered actions**, each one something an editor can do today. Bad: "improve
content". Good: "Add chunk C1 (pricing section, 4 price queries, 3,349 impressions) right after the intro."

## Order of operations

Go down this list and include the steps that apply, most impactful first.

1. **Match the SERP's intent and format.** If you can search, look at the top 5 for the main query. Are they
   service pages, guides, lists, comparison tables, tools? If the page is a different type (a thin service page
   competing with in-depth guides), say so first. Format mismatch beats every other fix.
2. **Cover the keyword gaps.** These are queries the page already ranks for but barely mentions (gaps.json).
   It's the highest-leverage content work because Google already connects the page to them. Reference the chunk IDs.
3. **Close the depth gap against the top 3.** Name the subtopics, questions, data, tables, images or steps they
   cover that this page doesn't. Word count is a symptom, not a goal: add what's missing, not filler.
4. **Internal links.** 3-5 contextual links from stronger pages (gaps.json -> link_sources, or high-click pages on
   related topics), with descriptive anchors that use the target query. For money pages also check that the
   page is reachable from navigation or the homepage.
5. **On-page basics.** Main query in the title, the H1 and the first 100 words. A direct answer or offer above the fold.
6. **Trust (E-E-A-T).** Author or practitioner bio, credentials, real photos, reviews or testimonials, case
   studies, a clear "last updated" date. Especially important for health, finance and legal (YMYL) topics.
7. **Fix cannibalisation.** If another page of the site competes for the same query (Cannibalisation tab), pick
   one owner: merge the weaker page into it with a 301, or de-optimise the weaker page and link it to the owner.
8. **Technical sanity.** Indexable, self-referencing canonical, not blocked, reasonable speed and mobile
   layout. Mention this only if you found a problem or couldn't check.
9. **Links from other sites** (competitive queries only): 2-3 relevant, earned links (local partners, suppliers,
   associations, a useful resource others will cite).
10. **After publishing**: request indexing in GSC (URL Inspection), then compare 28 days before vs 28 days after.

## Page 2 vs page 3

| | Page 2 (11-20) | Page 3 (21-30) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical problem | Missing subtopics, weak internal linking | Wrong format, thin content, or a stronger page on the site competing |
| Typical plan | Gap chunks + internal links + title/H1 tune | Restructure or expand substantially, then gap chunks + links |
| Expect | Movement within 2-6 weeks | Often 6-12 weeks, sometimes links from other sites needed |

## Diagnosis examples

> Ranks 11-24 for seven implant queries but has no cost section, even though "dental implants cost" alone brings
> 4,000+ impressions. At ~350 words it's much thinner than the guides ranking top 3, and only one blog post links
> to it.

> Page 3 for "root canal treatment" (3,100 impressions) with a 90-word page. Competitors answer "does it hurt",
> cost and recovery time on the same page. This needs an expansion, not tweaks.

## Self-check

- [ ] Every action names *what* to add and *where*, with chunk IDs or link sources where they exist
- [ ] Actions are ordered by impact
- [ ] Nothing generic ("optimise content", "improve SEO")
- [ ] Facts about competitors come from an actual search, or are left out
